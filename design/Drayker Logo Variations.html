Round 19 · the verdict · five typefaces, side by side

Mixed case wins. Capitals stay on the team, they are just not the primary.

My reading, with the symbol alongside: the globe is already a closed, circular, loud shape. In all caps the word repeats that compact rectangle and the whole ties — two blocks fighting for the same weight, and the name becomes a machine label. In mixed case the ascenders of D and k and the descender of y create their own contour: there is hierarchy, the symbol leads and the word follows. It is also more legible small — favicon with caption, footer, email signature.

Spaced capitals do not go in the bin: they are the technical version — plate, hull, report cover, footer band, where the word needs to become a ruler and not a name. Two functions, one clear hierarchy.

Round 18 · Drayker, mixed case

Only the D in capitals. No letter distorted.

Mixed case lowers the tone and solves the original problem: in lowercase the r, the k and the y are simple shapes — stem and diagonal — with no fancy little leg. And the text is no longer stretched: out went lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs" and the strong negative tracking, so each letter reaches the printer exactly as the type designer drew it.
